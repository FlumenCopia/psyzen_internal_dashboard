Why Phased?
7 Reasons Step-by-Step Wins
Committing ₹30L+ upfront to an unvalidated platform is how early-stage startups fail. Here is exactly why phasing is the professional and strategic choice.
💡
Reason 1
Capital Preservation — Spend Only What You Validate

Release budget per phase. Phase 0 branding costs ₹29,999. Phase 2 MVP costs ₹10–12L. If the MVP shows strong adoption, Phase 3–5 are funded by evidence — not hope. You don't need to build the whole hospital before the first patient walks in.

🏗️
Reason 2
Each Phase Validates the Next — Real-World Evidence

The MVP will generate real user behavior data — which consultation modes are used, which tier is most requested, where users drop off. This intelligence shapes Phase 3's AI features. Building voice before knowing if users prefer text is expensive guesswork.

📣
Reason 3
Marketing Before Platform — Build Audience First

Mental health is a new concept for most users in Kerala. A 2-month pre-launch marketing phase means Psyzen launches to an educated, waiting audience — not an empty waiting room. Most platforms skip this and spend 6 months explaining what they are while also trying to acquire users.

💰
Reason 4
Monthly Cash Flow vs. Lump-Sum Commitment

Instead of one ₹30L invoice, phasing converts investment into predictable monthly costs: ₹2–3.5L/month across 12 months. This is manageable within startup cash flow, allows EMI financing, and lets the business generate first revenue from beta clinics before the full build is complete.

⚖️
Reason 5
Legal and Compliance Complexity Requires Iteration

DPDP Act compliance, Telemedicine Practice Guidelines 2020, informed consent flows, and crisis detection liability are evolving requirements needing legal review. A phased build lets each compliance layer be reviewed before the next layer is added — rather than auditing 60,000 lines of code at launch.

🤖
Reason 6
AI Systems Need Iteration — Not Just Integration

A local LLM-powered mental health triage system cannot be built in 5 weeks and deployed to real patients. The AI needs prompt engineering cycles, clinical validation, safety testing, and hallucination audits. Phasing gives the team 3 months of MVP feedback before RAG and crisis detection go live.

🎯
Reason 7
Brand First — Platform Second

Psyzen is entering a trust-sensitive market. Users will not share their most vulnerable moments with a platform they've never heard of. Building brand identity and content reputation before the platform launches means users arrive with trust already established.

MVP Development — Core Platform Build
Month 3–7 · 20 Weeks · IT Team Lead
₹10L–12L IT + ₹3.25L FlumenX ▾

What This Phase Delivers: The core functional platform — patient onboarding, AI-assisted triage (Tiers 1–2), patient dashboard, doctor/social worker dashboard, appointment system, and DPDP-compliant consent management. By end of Phase 2, Psyzen can begin beta testing with real clinical partners and generate its first revenue. This is the most critical and largest phase of the entire build.

🏗️ Modules Built in This Phase

  • M1Authentication System: Custom user model with role fields (patient, social worker, psychologist, clinical psychologist, psychiatrist, admin). JWT + OTP verification. Role-based access control middleware.
  • M2Consent Management: DPDP-compliant multi-tier consent capture. Separate consent for: AI interaction, data sharing between tiers, session recording. Immutable consent audit log. Legal review required before any user testing.
  • M3AI Chat Engine (Tier 1-2): Local LLM integration via Ollama (LLaMA3 8B). System prompt with triage logic, safety rules, escalation triggers. Context window management (20 turns). Basic risk flagging to trigger human review.
  • M4Patient Dashboard: Tier status badge, risk level indicator, next appointment, chat interface (real-time WebSocket), session history with AI summaries, appointment booking calendar, profile + consent settings.
  • M5Doctor/Social Worker Dashboard: Assigned patient list with tier and risk levels, AI-generated alert panel, session review interface, clinical notes editor, appointment management, tier recommendation approval.
  • M6Appointment System: Calendar view, slot selection and booking, conflict detection, automatic reminders (WhatsApp + email), reschedule flow.
  • M7Platform Website v2: Full marketing site replacing teaser, complete service pages, blog live, SEO-optimized, waitlist converted to signup flow.

⚠️ Phase 2 Critical Decisions & Challenges

  • !Local LLM Server Setup: Client requested local LLM for DPDP privacy compliance. Ollama + LLaMA3 8B on private VPS (16GB+ RAM, GPU preferred). Budget: ₹15,000–25,000/month VPS. This must be confirmed in writing before first commit.
  • !Legal Consultation: DPDP consent flows must be reviewed by a healthcare legal professional before any user-facing testing.
  • !Clinical Advisor Engagement: A psychiatrist or clinical psychologist must review the triage logic, tier escalation triggers, and crisis response protocols before any patient interaction goes live.
  • !Video Consultation Scope: Client mentioned video as a consultation mode. The base proposal lists video as Phase 4. This needs to be confirmed — either in base scope or explicitly deferred.

3
AI Enhancement — Intelligence, RAG & Full 4-Tier Triage
Month 7–9 · 12 Weeks · IT Team + Clinical Advisor
₹6L–8L IT + ₹2.55L FlumenX ▾

What This Phase Delivers: The intelligence layer — RAG-powered knowledge retrieval, Personality Cluster A/B detection, full crisis detection system, all 4 tiers active, admin dashboard, and real-time emergency escalation. This is the phase that transforms Psyzen from an appointment-booking chatbot into a clinically intelligent triage platform. It requires a clinical advisor's active involvement throughout. Phase 3 is where the platform becomes genuinely defensible against competitors.

🧠 Modules Built in This Phase

  • M1RAG Knowledge Pipeline: Document ingestion (CBT protocols, DBT workbooks, clinical guidelines in PDF/DOCX). Text chunking with 500-char chunks. Local embeddings via SentenceTransformers (all-MiniLM-L6-v2 — no external API). ChromaDB vector store (self-hosted). Similarity search with top-5 retrieval. All patient data stays on your server.
  • M2Personality Cluster A/B Detection: Prompt-engineered conversation analysis to identify Cluster A (paranoid, schizoid, schizotypal) vs Cluster B (antisocial, borderline, histrionic, narcissistic) behavioral patterns. Outputs structured risk signals for clinical review — never a direct diagnosis. Validated by clinical advisor before live use.
  • M3Crisis Detection System: Multi-layer detection — Layer 1: keyword/phrase detection (fast, synchronous). Layer 2: LLM risk scoring (structured JSON output: LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH/CRITICAL). Layer 3: session history pattern analysis. CRITICAL trigger: conversation paused, psychiatrist/admin alerted via WebSocket, crisis resources displayed, incident logged. Must be validated by clinical advisor with 50+ scenario scripts before going live.
  • M4Full 4-Tier Escalation: Tier 1 (AI + Social Worker), Tier 2 (Social Worker + Psychologist), Tier 3 (Clinical Psychologist), Tier 4 (Psychiatrist + medication tracking). AI continuously reassesses tier assignment. Manual override by clinicians.
  • M5Admin Dashboard: Full platform overview, user management, AI monitoring, knowledge base management, emergency escalation control, audit logs, system analytics.
  • M6Analytics & Reporting: Patient activity analytics, risk trend dashboards, AI insight generation, clinical outcome tracking, progress reports for each patient.

🚨 Phase 3 Non-Negotiables

  • !Crisis Detection Liability Protocol: Before Phase 3 goes live, the legal response protocol must be documented — what happens if the psychiatrist doesn't respond to a CRITICAL alert within X minutes? Who bears liability? This requires legal documentation signed by Psyzen's operating entity.
  • !RAG Knowledge Curation: The CBT/DBT protocols, clinical guidelines, and crisis protocols in the knowledge base must be curated by a licensed mental health professional. Generic document upload produces generic — and potentially harmful — AI responses. This is not optional.
  • !Telemedicine Guidelines 2020 Compliance: All clinical interactions on the platform must comply with India's Telemedicine Practice Guidelines. Legal review before Phase 3 launch.

Brand Architecture
Building Trust in a Trust-Sensitive Market
Mental health is a category where brand trust outweighs any feature. A user will not share their most vulnerable moments with a platform they don't recognize. Every brand decision must earn that trust before asking for anything in return.
🧠
Brand Positioning
The Trusted Guide, Not the Replacement

Psyzen is not a therapy replacement. It is the intelligent guide that helps people understand their mental health and find the right level of human support. Core positioning: "Your first step toward the right care." This reduces stigma, lowers the barrier to entry, and positions AI as an enabler of human care — not a substitute for it.

🎨
Visual Direction
Calm Authority — Not Clinical, Not Casual

Avoid cold hospital white + clinical blue (intimidating). Avoid bright pastels + playful icons (trivialises mental health). The recommended direction: deep teal + warm cream + measured gold accents. Soft geometric forms. Humanist typeface for warmth, serif for authority. Think: a trusted professional who actually listens.

Go-to-Market Strategy
Education → Desire → Funnel
Mental health requires education before conversion. The strategy runs in 3 stages that cannot be reversed or skipped. Creating the category in the user's mind must happen before selling the product to them.

The Core Challenge: You can't run "Book a therapy session" ads to people who don't believe they need therapy and don't understand what triage means. The first 3 months of marketing are entirely about creating the category, not selling the product.

What We Are Actually Building

Psyzen is not a website. It is not a chatbot. It is a full-stack clinical-grade AI platform with a local privacy-first LLM, a 4-tier medical escalation system, real-time crisis detection, multi-modal consultation (text, voice, video), RAG-powered knowledge retrieval, consent management, audit logging, and a multi-role dashboard system for patients and 5 clinical roles. This is the kind of platform that healthcare enterprises spend ₹2–5Cr building with large teams. We are doing it in 12 months at startup speed, without sacrificing clinical safety.

Why This Investment Is Justified
You Are Not Paying for a Website. You Are Paying for Clinical Infrastructure.
Every line item in this budget exists because the platform requires it — not because it is convenient. Here is the honest breakdown of complexity and cost drivers.
🤖
Cost Driver 1
Local LLM — Privacy by Architecture

Running a local LLM (Ollama + LLaMA3 8B) instead of OpenAI means patient mental health conversations never leave your server — which is the correct approach for DPDP compliance. But it requires a dedicated GPU server (₹15,000–30,000/month), custom integration work, prompt engineering for clinical safety, and ongoing tuning. OpenAI would be faster to build — but legally and ethically wrong for this use case.

🚨
Cost Driver 2
Crisis Detection — Clinical-Grade Safety

Building an AI that reliably detects suicidal ideation, self-harm indicators, and psychiatric emergencies is one of the hardest NLP engineering challenges. It requires a multi-layer detection system (keyword, LLM scoring, history analysis), clinical advisor validation with 50+ scenarios, a real-time alert pipeline, and a legal liability protocol. A system that misses a crisis can cause patient harm. This cannot be cut short.

🏥
Cost Driver 3
4-Tier Clinical Escalation — Not a Simple Chatbot

The platform routes patients through 4 distinct clinical tiers — AI + Social Worker → Psychologist → Clinical Psychologist → Psychiatrist — with dynamic reassessment, tier override, and consent re-capture at each escalation. Each tier has its own dashboard, data permissions, and workflow logic. This is not a chat interface. It is a multi-role clinical workflow system.

🔒
Cost Driver 4
RAG Pipeline — Medical Knowledge Retrieval

The RAG system ingests CBT/DBT protocols, clinical guidelines, and crisis response documents, converts them to vector embeddings, stores them in ChromaDB, and retrieves relevant context for every AI response. Building a healthcare-grade RAG pipeline with hallucination guards and clinical validation is significantly more complex than a general-purpose chatbot RAG. Every retrieved response must be safe, contextually accurate, and clinically appropriate.

⚖️
Cost Driver 5
DPDP Compliance + Consent Architecture

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 requires specific, granular, freely-given consent for sensitive health data. For Psyzen, this means separate consent capture for: general platform use, AI interaction, data sharing between clinical tiers, session recording, and analytics. Every consent event must be logged immutably. The consent management module alone is a significant engineering investment — and it must be reviewed by a healthcare legal professional before go-live.

🎙️
Cost Driver 6
Voice + Video — Multi-Modal Clinical Sessions

Voice consultation requires a complete STT → AI → TTS → audio pipeline with sub-2-second latency. Self-hosting Whisper for STT and Coqui TTS for speech output ensures privacy but adds engineering complexity. Video consultation using Daily.co or Agora SDK adds real-time WebRTC infrastructure. Both require separate consent flows, session recording management, and integration with the clinical notes system.
